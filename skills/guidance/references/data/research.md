## Peer-reviewed studies

Found 2026-11-15, first from the published abstracts. Rows marked "read" were checked against the paper on 2026-11-17; `sources.md` lists which papers were read and how far. Tier A: 400+ people or field data at scale; tier B: smaller controlled experiments.

| What | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thumb target size | error rates stop improving at 9.6mm (single taps) and 7.7mm (taps in sequence); recommended 9.2mm and 9.6mm. 20 right-handed people (17 men), standing, HP iPAQ PDA with an 8.9cm screen, lift-off selection. Single taps: centre rated most comfortable (5.7 of 7), the two left corners least (3.7); authors name the left side and bottom-right corner as hardest from ratings and tap scatter together. Taps in sequence: top right most comfortable, bottom right least. No main effect of location on errors; one size by location interaction in the sequence phase (p = .039). Full text read 2026-11-17 | SCI-THUMB (B) |
| Tap accuracy at scale | 120,626,225 touch events, 91,731 installs: error rate varies with size and screen location; touches systematically skewed; a correction cut errors by 7.79% | SCI-TAPS (A) |
| Swipe vs tap | swipe targets: longer times, more errors, shifted touch points | SCI-SWIPE (B) |
| First impression speed | complexity and prototypicality affect appeal within 17 to 50ms; low complexity plus familiar layout most appealing; low prototypicality rated unattractive at any complexity; linear, not U-shaped, relation with complexity. Both factors act within 17ms, complexity more strongly at that exposure. Two studies, 59 people (mainly psychology students in Basel) and 82 people, screenshots of company websites, passive viewing. Paper read 2026-11-17 (methods, results and limitations) | SCI-VC-PT (B) |
| What drives appeal | model of complexity, colourfulness and demographics explains 48% of variance in appeal after 500ms; complexity far stronger than colourfulness. Appeal experiment: 242 people (184 and 122 in the colourfulness and complexity experiments; 548 in all), 450 sites. Sample more educated and multinational than typical users. Full text read 2026-11-17 | SCI-FIRSTIMP (B) |
| Preferences by audience | 2.4 million ratings of 430 sites, 39,975 people from 179 countries, 500ms exposure (1,542,166 ratings from 32,222 people after cleaning; country analysis on 43 countries, 43% of it from the US). Inverted U: peak appeal at complexity 4.2 of 10 and colourfulness 6.1 of 9; high complexity costs more than low (Cohen's d 2.0 against 0.6). Peaks range 2.5 to 4.8 (complexity) and 3.6 to 7.6 (colourfulness) by age, gender, education and country. Full text read 2026-11-17 | SCI-PREF (A) |
| Highest-rated factors | symmetry, low complexity, blue hue, medium brightness, medium to high saturation; structure outweighs colour (194 people) | SCI-STRUCT (B) |
| Reading font size | sizes 10, 12, 14, 18, 22, 26 in Arial on a 17-inch desktop monitor at about 60cm; fixation duration fell steadily to 18 and was lowest at 22; comprehension lower at 10 and 12 than at 18; 26 rated less readable than 18; at least 18 recommended for website body text. 104 people, 93 analysed for fixations. Sizes called points but set in CSS; browser default of 16 also called points. Full text read 2026-11-17 | SCI-FONTSIZE (B) |
| Line spacing | study units 0.8, 1.0, 1.4, 1.8 = 96%, 120%, 168%, 216% of the font size (CSS `line-height` 0.96, 1.2, 1.68, 2.16). No effect on fixation duration; comprehension scores lower at 0.96 than at the other three; self-rated comprehension lower at 2.16 than at 1.2 | SCI-FONTSIZE (B) |
| Font choice | 35% reading speed difference between a reader's fastest and slowest font, comprehension unchanged; 14% faster in fastest than in preferred font; preference and familiarity do not predict speed. 352 US crowdworkers after filtering (500 recruited), each read in five of 16 fonts, sizes normalised, English. Paper read 2026-11-17 (methods, results and limitations) | SCI-FONTS (B) |
| Letter and word spacing | character -0.1em or 0.4em and word -0.2em or 1.5em drop speed, comprehension and preference (102 and 113 readers) | SCI-SPACING (B) |
| Dyslexia | larger text and character spacing read faster, with and without dyslexia (92 people) | SCI-DYSLEXIA (B) |
| Light vs dark text | light mode read reliably faster, glance and paragraph (459 people); paragraph readers did not prefer it | SCI-POLARITY-READ (A) |
| Why light reads faster | brighter screen; no advantage at equal luminance; independent of room light; larger at small sizes; holds for older adults | SCI-POLARITY, SCI-LUMINANCE, SCI-POL-SIZE, SCI-POL-AGE (B) |
| Charts light vs dark | no average difference between modes; per person, mean percent difference in time 0.32 (under 60) and 0.34 (60 and over), similar to the effect of chart type; preferred mode often not the best one. 135 recruited, 104 analysed (52 and 52), US crowdworkers on desktop, 24 trials each, short paper. Full text read 2026-11-17 | SCI-VIS-POL (B) |
| Wait feedback | online, 425 US participants, 3x3 between subjects (no display, elapsed, remaining; 10, 30, 60s), plain numeric timers, no progress bar. Frustration: remaining 30.7 against elapsed 24.1 on a 0 to 100 scale (p = .019, small effect). Pleasantness: remaining 5.33 against elapsed 5.67 of 7. Perceived duration: no display 4.06 against remaining 3.70 of 7. No difference in task performance. Authors: context-sensitive considerations, not prescriptive rules. Full text read 2026-11-17 | SCI-WAIT-CLOCK (A) |
| Loading animation | animated beats static on perceived wait, four experiments of 198 to 411 people; weaker under high task involvement or practical goals (still significant, p about .04). 15-second waits, a moving indicator against the same one frozen (rotating ring in study 1; moving dots beside a three-step progress bar, shown in both versions, in studies 2 and 4; cycling dots and a small ring in study 3), US crowdworkers on desktop, about 100 to 205 per cell. Paper read 2026-11-17 (methods, results and limitations) | SCI-WAIT-ANIM (A) |
| Form error timing | errors after the whole form best, immediate worst (77 and 90 people) | SCI-FORM-ERR (B) |
| Form guidelines combined | 19 of 20 guidelines applied to three German-language newspaper registration forms; 65 people, lab, 32 on originals and 33 on improved. First-attempt submissions: 10, 9, 22 of 32 on originals against 24, 24, 28 of 33 on improved. Completion time 25%, 33% and 18% shorter (third not significant). Ratings up to 16% higher. Users named format specifications and required-field marking as most important. Full text read 2026-11-17 | SCI-FORMS (B) |
| Cost of breaking guidelines | low contrast and unclear links slow people and raise search effort; icon changes had mixed, context-dependent effects. 102 computer science students, 11 static website mock-ups in compliant and degraded versions, Material Design 2 guidelines. Paper read 2026-11-17 (methods, results and limitations) | SCI-GUIDE-COST (B) |
| Light vs dark by character size | proofreading at 8, 10, 12, 14pt (0.22 to 0.34 degrees at 50cm), dark room; light-mode advantage grows linearly as size falls. Polarity between subjects; the dark group reported more blur and more difficulty moving between lines, other ratings (such as ability to focus) did not differ. 165 volunteers, 160 analysed, aged 18 to 44, German. Article read 2026-11-17 for methods, results and limitations, in the first author's dissertation | SCI-POL-SIZE (B) |
